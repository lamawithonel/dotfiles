## Copy

Hold it. Not look at it-- hold it. Feel the weight settle into your palm the way a house key settles when you've come home more times than you can count.

This is not a pen. This is the last object your grandfather's hand ever closed around before he signed his name for the final time, and someone, somewhere, kept it in a drawer for forty years because throwing it away felt like throwing him away twice.

This is not a pen. This is the scratch against paper that once sounded like a story being told just to you-- a birthday card, a note tucked into a lunch bag, a signature at the bottom of a letter you have read so many times the folds have gone soft as cloth.

This is not a pen. It is the smell of ink and the particular hush of a kitchen table at eleven at night, someone writing because the phone felt too loud for what they needed to say.

Somewhere, right now, there is a drawer holding the pen that mattered. One day yours will sit in a drawer too.

Someone will hold it. Not look at it-- hold it.

And they will remember you by the weight of it.
