## Copy

Hold it. Feel the weight settle into your palm.

This is not a tool. This is a decision, made new, each time you press it down.

The world types. The world swipes. The world forgets the sound a nib makes against paper -- that scratch, that small confession nobody else can hear.

You will not forget it.

Pick it up. Take the cap off. Hear it click.

There is a version of you who still writes things down. Who still signs a name and means it. Who still drafts the letter never sent, the apology, the resignation, the vow -- and lets it stand.

That version is waiting.

Ink does not forgive. Ink does not blink. Ink commits, the way you used to commit, before everything turned reversible, before every word came pre-loaded with an undo.

Write the sentence. Cross it out if you must -- but see the crossing. Own it. Nothing digital lets you own a mistake the way one dragged line of ink does.

This is the last honest object left on your desk. Everything else negotiates. This does not.

Write something down. Write something true.

Buy the pen.
