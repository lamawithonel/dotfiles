## Copy

This is not a pen. Stop calling it a pen.

Look-- feel the weight of it, the small black gravity of it, resting between your fingers like a held breath, like a decision not yet made. You have held cheap plastic in your hand and called it a tool. You have signed leases with it, breakups with it, resignation letters full of words you didn't mean. This is different. This is not a tool. This is an accelerant.

Ink is not ink. Ink is intention made liquid, made portable, made permanent the second it touches the page, and there is no un-signing, no take-back, no soft cursor to delete the truth of what you meant. You press. The world changes. That is the whole of it, and it has always been the whole of it, since the first hand pressed the first mark into the first wet clay and said: I was here. I meant this.

Hold it. Hear the click of it like a door closing on every excuse you've ever made for yourself. Feel the ink move like a low current, a hum under the skin, the taste of iron and resolve at the back of your throat -- yes, taste it, because this is that kind of pen, the kind that crosses wires in the brain until sound and smell and certainty are the same signal firing at once.

You will use this pen to sign the contract you have been avoiding. You will use it to write the name you haven't said out loud yet. You will use it, and then you will remember this moment -- not as the moment you bought a pen, but as the moment before everything else started, the hinge the whole rest of your life turned on.

Every pen writes words. This one writes decisions. Pens run out. Decisions don't. That's the whole pitch. That's the whole reason you're still holding it.

So-- go on. Sign something.
