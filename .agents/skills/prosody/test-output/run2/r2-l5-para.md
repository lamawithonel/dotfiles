## Copy

Take the pen. Not a pen. The pen.

Steel core. Matte black. Weight dead center. No wobble. No give. Hold it. Feel that. That is not tolerance. That is intention.

Ink flows. Ink does not skip. Ink does not hesitate. It smells faintly of decisions made. Neither do you.

The nib is cold at first touch. Then it warms. Then it is just your hand, moving, sure.

Write your name. Write it again. Write it a third time. The name stops looking like a word. The name starts looking like a fact.

Papers get signed. Contracts get signed. Vows get signed. A whole life turns on a signature. A signature needs a spine. This pen has a spine.

Everyone forgets. Everyone. The birthday. The apology. The name of the nurse who was kind at 3 a.m. People forget everything, eventually. Paper does not.

Write it down.

The tip touches paper. Paper remembers. Paper holds the hour. Paper holds the ache. Paper holds the decision, long after the hand goes quiet. That is the whole trade. Ink for permanence. Six grams of steel for a piece of you that outlives you.

Hear that click. That is not plastic. That is a door closing. Every excuse for waiting -- gone.

You meant to write the letter. You meant to sign the lease. You meant to leave the note on the counter, the one that says what you actually meant, all those years. Meaning is not doing. This is doing.

One pen. One hand. One line. Then another. Then another. The page fills. The day gets accounted for. The world gets proof you were in it.

This is not for how it writes. This is for what gets left behind. Someday someone holds a piece of paper with your hand on it. That will be the only part of you left in the room.

Everything else fades. The signature stays.
