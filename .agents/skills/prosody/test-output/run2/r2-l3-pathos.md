## Copy

This isn't a pen. Not really. It's the thing your grandfather kept in his shirt pocket through forty years of Sunday letters, the weight that made his handwriting slow down enough to mean something. A pen is just ink and a nib until someone trusts it with the truth-- then it becomes the last object standing between what you feel and whether anyone will ever know you felt it.

Hold this one a moment. Feel how it settles into the curve of your hand like it already knows you-- the small click of the cap, the faint smell of the drawer where the good pens always lived, tucked beside the birthday cards no one threw away. There's a pause every time you first touch nib to paper, a held breath before the first word arrives. That pause is where the pen does its real work: not writing, but letting you become, for one unhurried minute, someone worth writing to.

Because in the end we don't remember what was typed. We remember whose hand wrote it.
