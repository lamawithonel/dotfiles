## Copy

Not a pen. A last analog act.

Every day you touch a hundred surfaces that forget you the instant you lift
your hand -- glass, aluminum, glow. Nothing holds. Nothing keeps the shape of
your hand.

This does.

Feel the weight of it first -- brass through lacquer, cold, then warm,
because you are holding it. Uncap it. Listen: no chime, no notification, just
the small confident click of a thing built to be used, not charged.

Now write your name.

Not typed. Not autocorrected into someone smoother than you. Pressed -- ink
dragged by the same hand that once signed a lease, a card, a name on a
hospital form, because some moments still refuse to be pixels.

This is the pen your daughter will find in a drawer in thirty years and know,
before she reads a single word, that it was yours.

This is the pen under the contract that changed everything, the pen that ran
dry mid-signature and made you laugh, the pen you lend and always, always ask
back.

You do not remember which stylus you used last Tuesday.

You will remember this one.

Hold it. Sign something that matters. Keep it in your pocket like a promise
you intend to survive you --

because some things are not meant to be deleted.

They are meant to be handed down.
