# Prosody and Rhetorical Architecture: a Poetic Writing Guide

Default to prose.  Most requests that reach this guide are pitches, copy,
framing, or emotive prose -- not verse.  Compose in metrical form only when
verse is asked for explicitly or the material is unmistakably a poem.  The
prosodic devices below carry the emotional load in either mode.

## Dynamics

How hard to drive the material.  Set this before composing.  Dynamics govern
every section that follows: they decide how much of The Pull runs, how many
devices stack, and how far the register is pushed.

| Level | Use for | Length | Device density |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 -- Plain | Buttons, subject lines, headlines, a single claim | 1 sentence | 0-1 devices |
| 2 -- Tempered | Taglines, short body copy, a caption with weight | 2-4 sentences | 2 devices |
| 3 -- Full | Pitches, landing copy, presentation beats.  **Default.** | 1-3 paragraphs | 3-4 devices |
| 4 -- Grand / Forte | Keynote closes, manifestos, launch films, eulogies | 150-300 words | 5-6 devices, stacked |
| 5 -- Fortissimo / Con Furia | Material meant to land before it is analyzed | 300+ words, no cap | 7+ devices; every ladder at its maximum |

What escalates, level 1 through 5:

* **Repetition** -- none -> incidental echo -> anaphora across sentences ->
  a refrain the reader starts to anticipate -> sustained incantatory cadence.
* **Address** -- third person -> one "you" -> "you" throughout -> imperatives
  -> the reader's future stated as settled fact.
* **Sensory load** -- none -> one concrete particular -> two channels ->
  layered detail -> senses crossing into each other.
* **The turn** -- no volta -> one turn -> one marked turn -> several ->
  continuous turning, never letting the reader settle.
* **Withholding** -- resolve immediately -> one held beat -> a caesura the
  reader feels -> resolution deferred across paragraphs -> resolution the
  reader must supply themselves.

These ladders also decide which architectures are available.  An architecture
whose spec requires a move the level forbids cannot be used at that level:
level 1 permits no turn, so nothing built on a *volta* will fit there.  When
the level and the architecture conflict, the level wins -- change the
architecture, not the budget.

### Selecting the Level

Infer from the surface of the ask: its length, its stakes, and the temperature
of the words used to make it.  "Give me a tagline" is 1 or 2.  "Write the
pitch" is 3.  "Make them cry", "this is the closing slide", or "go all out"
is 4 or higher.

When genuinely unsure, choose one level lower.  The failure modes are not
symmetric.  Under-driven copy merely underperforms; over-driven copy reads
as insincere, and a reader who catches the machinery working stops trusting
the writer for the rest of the piece.

Honor an explicit request over any inference: "dial it up", "dial it back",
"keep it plain", "level 4", "go all out".

### The Ceiling

Level 5 is loud enough that an attentive reader can hear the writing working.
That is fine when the audience is already leaning in -- a manifesto, a vow,
a keynote close, a tribute to someone who matters.  It inverts on a cold or
skeptical audience, and it inverts hardest wherever the writer's credibility
is the thing actually being sold.  Match the dynamics to how far the reader
has already opened the door.

## The Pull

The procedure for prose that evokes yearning.  Run it in order.  At level 1
run step 1 alone; at level 2 run steps 1, 4, and 5; at level 3 and above run
all five.

1. **Name the ache, not the object.**  Every request has a stated subject
   (a product, a decision, a person) and an unstated hunger underneath it:
   belonging, safety, being known, time recovered, the self the reader meant
   to become.  Write to the hunger.  The subject is only the door.
2. **Enter through the concrete.**  Open on one physical particular -- a
   smell, a doorway, a hand, a sound at a specific hour.  Abstractions cannot
   be missed.  Only particulars can be missed, and missing is the engine.
3. **Withhold.**  Hold the resolution one beat past comfort.  Yearning is
   the distance between reach and grasp; close it early and it evaporates.
   Structural silence, a caesura, or a truncated line does this work.
4. **Pivot inward (*volta*).**  Turn from the object to what it costs or
   restores in the reader.  This turn is the single highest-leverage move
   in the guide; every form below marks a place for it.
5. **Land universal and land short.**  The final sentence is the shortest.
   Resolve on a common human experience, then stop.  The silence after the
   last line is part of the composition.

## Device Kit

Cross-cutting moves.  Usable in plain prose.  Each names the form that drives
it hardest, if the effect is worth escalating into verse.

* **Function-to-artifact reframe** -- restate a technical or functional thing
  as a psychological one.  Opens the pathos register.
* **Volta / emotional pivot** -- redirect from objective features toward
  memory, longing, or belonging.  See Petrarchan Sonnet, Tanka.
* **Parataxis** -- stack short independent clauses, no subordinating
  conjunctions.  Projects certainty.  See Paratactic Modernist Cadence.
* **Caesura** -- a hard pause mid-flow, forcing each assertion to land alone.
  Pairs with parataxis.
* **Enjambment** -- run syntax past the line or clause break so nothing settles.
  Produces breathlessness.  See Enjambed Heroic Couplets.
* **Truncation / catalexis** -- cut the expected final beat.  The absence
  reads as an ache or as urgency.  See Truncated Ballad, Trochaic Tetrameter
  Catalectic.
* **Juxtaposition cut (*kireji*)** -- set two images side by side and refuse
  to explain the relation.  See Haiku.
* **Aphoristic lock** -- close on a short, self-contained, universally framed
  maxim.  See Epigrammatic Assertion, Shakespearean Sonnet.

## Prose Architectures

Whole-piece shapes for non-metrical work.  These carry the common case.
A move stack from the index below tells you which devices to use; an
architecture tells you how to build the entire piece around them.

### Epigrammatic Aphoristic Assertion

* **Trigger Context:** High-stakes persuasion, establishing instant authority,
  shifting framed narratives, or delivering memorable, conclusive declarations.
* **Mechanical Execution:**
  * Construct short, highly memorable, self-contained declarative maxims
    structured as universal truths.
  * Reframe broad or abstract concepts into direct, engineered human constructs.
  * Employ parallel syntactic structures (e.g., *"If you don't like [X],
    change [Y]"*) to project unshakeable authority and force a complete shift
    in perspective.

### Pathos-Dominant Nostalgia Architecture

* **Trigger Context:** Deep emotional resonance, memory activation, high-impact
  persuasive presentations, or profound personal alignment.
* **Mechanical Execution:**
  * Open by reframing a functional or technical concept into a psychological
    or emotional artifact.
  * Execute an emotional pivot (*volta*) that redirects focus away from
    objective features toward internal memory, longing, or belonging.
  * Anchor the conclusion on a deeply personal, universal human experience
    (e.g., the quiet ache of memory, the safety of returning home, or feeling
    understood).

### Paratactic Modernist Cadence

* **Trigger Context:** Crisp, unshakeable assertions, modern executive copy,
  or rhythmic, understated persuasion.
* **Mechanical Execution:**
  * Stack short, simple, independent clauses sequentially without subordinating
    conjunctions (*parataxis*).
  * Rely on stark, bold nouns and concise adjectives arranged in deliberate
    rhythmic beats.
  * Incorporate strategic structural pauses (cadenced pauses or caesuras)
    between brief statements to force the audience to fully absorb each
    assertion individually.

## Register Index

Select by the feeling to be produced, not by the form.  Take the move stack
for prose.  Take the form column only if composing verse.  Blending two
adjacent registers is permitted when the material genuinely sits between
them; blending three is a sign the register was never chosen.

| Target feeling | Prose move stack | Verse form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yearning, nostalgia, being known | Function-to-artifact reframe -> volta -> universal landing | Pathos-Dominant Nostalgia (above), Tanka[^jp], Keatsian Ode[^west] |
| Conviction, instant authority | Parallel syntax -> aphoristic lock | Epigrammatic Assertion (above), Shakespearean Sonnet[^west] |
| Calm, unshakeable assertion | Parataxis -> caesura -> stark nouns | Paratactic Modernist Cadence (above) |
| Urgency, incantation, pressure | Front-loaded stress -> catalexis | Trochaic Tetrameter Catalectic[^west] |
| Intimacy, closeness, sway | Rolling triple rhythm -> soft endings | Anapestic Lyric[^west], Sedōka[^jp] |
| Awe, immersion, luxury | Sensory density -> stretched final beat | Spenserian Stanza[^west], Keatsian Ode[^west] |
| Enchantment, held attention | Hypnotic repetition -> truncated silence | Truncated Ballad Stanza[^west] |
| Wit, rapport, knowing banter | Rolling cadence -> couplet punchline | Ottava Rima[^west] |
| Momentum, inevitability | Chained clauses -> heavy enjambment | Terza Rima[^west], Enjambed Heroic Couplets[^west] |
| Stillness, a captured moment | One particular -> juxtaposition cut | Haiku[^jp], Renku[^jp] |
| Devotion, fixation, longing | Sustained single subject -> late volta | Petrarchan Sonnet[^west] |
| Sustained tribute, recollection | Elaborate painting -> distilled envoy | Chōka[^jp] |

## Composing Verse

When a form is selected, read the one reference that owns it and execute
its specification exactly.  Line counts, meter, and rhyme schemes are hard
constraints.  Each reference is self-contained; do not read the others.  Use
`grep -A11 '^## {{Verse-Form}}' <file>` to get exactly one verse form.
